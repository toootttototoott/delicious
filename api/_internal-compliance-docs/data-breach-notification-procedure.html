Internal Procedure

Data Breach Notification Procedure

Version 1.0 | Effective date: 9 July 2026

1. Purpose

This procedure sets out how the business will decide whether notifications are required after a suspected or confirmed data breach and how those notifications will be prepared, approved, issued, and recorded.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to breaches involving guest booking data, booking enquiries, user account records, exported reports, backups, and service-provider incidents affecting the booking platform.

3. Trigger for notification assessment

Start this procedure as soon as a breach is suspected to involve personal information or where there is a reasonable possibility that unauthorised access, disclosure, loss, or compromise has occurred.

4. Assessment questions

  • What happened and when was it first detected?
  • What personal information was involved?
  • How many individuals may be affected?
  • Was the information encrypted, deleted, recovered, or otherwise protected?
  • Is serious harm to affected individuals likely?
  • Does the Privacy Act and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme apply to this incident?

5. Decision maker

The incident lead and business/privacy lead should prepare the notification recommendation. Final approval to notify customers, venues, or regulators should be given by the responsible business owner or authorised delegate.

6. Notification timing

Where notification is required, prepare and issue it as soon as practicable after the assessment is complete and the relevant threshold has been met. Do not delay necessary notifications longer than needed to confirm facts, contain the incident, and prepare accurate communications.

7. Who may need to be notified

  • Affected guests or other affected individuals.
  • Affected venue customers or business clients.
  • The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, where required.
  • Insurers, legal advisers, managed providers, or relevant authorities, where appropriate.

8. Required notification content

  • A short description of the incident.
  • The date or time window of the breach.
  • The kinds of personal information involved.
  • What the business has already done to contain and respond.
  • What affected individuals should do next.
  • How to contact the business for support or follow-up.

9. Communication channels

Use the most appropriate communication channel available for the incident, such as direct email, phone contact for high-risk cases, venue coordination, website notice, or regulator submission. Use direct notice where contact details are available and it is practical to do so.

10. Template process

  1. Prepare a draft notification using the current incident facts.
  2. Review the draft for accuracy, tone, and legal/regulatory completeness.
  3. Approve the final version.
  4. Issue the notification through the selected channel.
  5. Record when, how, and to whom the notice was sent.

11. Record keeping

Keep a copy of each notification, approval record, issue log, regulator submission, and any follow-up questions or complaints arising from the breach communication.

12. Review after notification

After notifications are completed, review whether any further updates are needed for affected individuals, whether FAQs or venue guidance should be published, and whether the incident requires changes to code, hosting, access controls, training, or vendor management.
